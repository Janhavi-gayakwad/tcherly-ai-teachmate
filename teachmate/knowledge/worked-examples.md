# Worked examples before practice
summary: Show a fully solved example step by step, explaining why each step is taken, before students solve similar problems.
helps_with: "Not enough explanation / examples"; "Content is too difficult"; novices meeting a new procedure (for example a new program or algorithm)
source: Sweller, J. & Cooper, G. A. (1985). The use of worked examples as a substitute for problem solving in learning algebra. Cognition and Instruction, 2(1); Atkinson, R. K., Derry, S. J., Renkl, A. & Wortham, D. (2000). Learning from examples: Instructional principles from the worked examples research. Review of Educational Research, 70(2).

## When it can help
Research on worked examples shows that novices learn new procedures better from studying solved examples than from solving problems straight away, because it reduces cognitive load.

## Try it
Before running or demonstrating a complex program, walk through one complete example slowly, naming the purpose of each step. Then show a second, slightly different example.

## How to tell if it helped
In the segment, the reasons "Not enough explanation / examples" and "Content is too difficult" should fall.
