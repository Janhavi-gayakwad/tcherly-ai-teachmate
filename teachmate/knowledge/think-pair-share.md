# Think-pair-share
summary: Students think about a question alone, discuss it with a partner, then pairs share with the class.
helps_with: in-class follow-up on a difficult segment; quiet classes; checking understanding of a concept many students found difficult
source: Lyman, F. (1981). The responsive classroom discussion: The inclusion of all students. In A. Anderson (Ed.), Mainstreaming Digest. University of Maryland, College Park.

## When it can help
Useful in the live class after a video, to revisit the part where Difficult clicks peaked. Thinking alone first gives everyone time to form an answer; the pair step lets students test their reasoning before speaking to the whole class.

## Try it
Pose one question about the difficult segment. Give a minute to think, a couple of minutes to discuss in pairs, then ask a few pairs to share and work through misconceptions together.

## How to tell if it helped
Listen for the misconceptions that surface. In the next video on related content, Difficult reasons such as "Not enough explanation / examples" should fall.
