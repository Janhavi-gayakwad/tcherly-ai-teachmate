# Muddiest point
summary: Ask students to write down, anonymously, the one thing that is still least clear to them.
helps_with: finding out what exactly was difficult when reasons are vague or missing; low participation in feedback
source: Mosteller, F. (1989). The "Muddiest Point in the Lecture" as a feedback device. On Teaching and Learning: The Journal of the Harvard-Danforth Center, 3; Angelo, T. A. & Cross, K. P. (1993). Classroom Assessment Techniques (2nd ed.). Jossey-Bass.

## When it can help
When Tcherly shows a difficult segment but students gave few reasons, a muddiest-point question in the next class reveals the specific confusion.

## Try it
At the start or end of the next class, ask "What was the muddiest point in the video about this topic?" and collect short written answers.

## How to tell if it helped
The answers should point to a specific step or term you can address directly.
