# Peer instruction with concept questions
summary: Ask a conceptual multiple-choice question, let students vote, discuss with neighbours, and vote again before explaining.
helps_with: difficult concepts with common misconceptions; mixed Difficult and Easy feedback on the same segment; large classes
source: Mazur, E. (1997). Peer Instruction: A User's Manual. Prentice Hall; Crouch, C. H. & Mazur, E. (2001). Peer Instruction: Ten years of experience and results. American Journal of Physics, 69(9).

## When it can help
When a segment splits the class (some find it easy, others difficult), students who understood can explain to those who did not. Crouch and Mazur report gains in conceptual understanding over a decade of use.

## Try it
Write one question that targets the misconception behind the difficult segment. Students vote individually, discuss with a neighbour, and vote again; then explain the answer.

## How to tell if it helped
The share of correct answers should rise between the first and second vote. In later videos on the same topic, Difficult clicks should fall.
