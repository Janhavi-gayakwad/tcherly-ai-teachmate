## Task: write a lesson report

Write a concise report the teacher can keep and share. It summarises how students experienced one video lecture and what the teacher has reflected on so far. It should fit on one or two printed pages.

This is not a conversation. All the evidence has already been gathered and is listed below, so you cannot call tools. Use only the evidence items given.

### Rules

- Every number, and every claim about the class, cites the evidence item it comes from, e.g. "net engagement fell to −6 in minute 21 [E7]". Use only IDs from the list below. Never invent, re-round or convert numbers. Reason percentages are shares of *reason selections*, not of students.
- Keep the reflective stance of your system instructions. Describe what the data shows, offer readings as possibilities, and never write "you should", "you must" or verdicts like "this lecture is bad".
- Mention what worked as well as what didn't.
- Write about the class as a group, never about individual students.
- Write for a busy teacher: short sentences, plain words, no filler or introductions. Keep each item to 1–3 sentences.
- Times are video minutes: "min 16-22" means 16:00–22:00.
- Viewing-behaviour evidence (where students jumped back to re-watch, skipped ahead, or stopped watching) shows what students did, not why. Mention it in a key moment when a re-watching or skipping hotspot falls inside that window, as supporting evidence, never as proof. Don't interpret pause counts, because every feedback click pauses the video.

### Sections

- **headline**: the single most important finding, in 1–2 sentences.
- **at_a_glance**: 3–5 short facts covering participation, the overall pattern, and the strongest positive and negative moments.
- **key_moments**: one entry for each time window listed under "Key moments selected", in time order. Keep the window exactly as given. `title` is a short neutral label for what happened, not a judgement. `students_said` covers the reasons and students' own words for that window; leave it empty if no reasons were given.
- **what_worked**: 1–3 points about moments students found engaging or clear, and why, according to the evidence.
- **reflections**: the teacher's saved bookmarks, questions and actions, summarised faithfully in the teacher's own terms. If none are saved, write a single item saying so.
- **open_questions**: 2–4 open, specific reflective questions the teacher could explore next. Connect them to the evidence and to the teacher's own reflections where there are any.
- **strategies**: 2–3 small, concrete options the teacher could adopt, adapt or reject. Base each one on a "Teaching strategy" evidence item where one fits: name the strategy, cite it, and credit its source in plain words (e.g. "worked examples (Atkinson et al.)"). For each, `addresses` says which finding it responds to (cited), and `check` says how the teacher could tell next time whether it helped (for example, which feedback to watch in that window). If a "possibly related reflection from another lesson" really addresses the same kind of problem, connect an option to it; otherwise ignore it.
